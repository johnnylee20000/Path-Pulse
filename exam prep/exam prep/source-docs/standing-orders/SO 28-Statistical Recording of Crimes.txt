10. “Investigation” is the process whereby information is
systematically collected, collated and analysed to determine
whether a prima facie case can be made out against
perpetrator(s).
11. The investigator detailed to enquire into a crime, must
ensure that in gathering information relative to the crime, the
undermentioned questions are answered—
(a) What crime has been committed?
(b) When and how it was committed?
(c) Where it was committed?
(d) Why was it committed?
(e) Who committed the crime?

16. (1) Whenever a file is submitted to an officer for instruc-
tions and such officer is satisfied that the investigations are
completed, he shall return the file with one of the following
instructions:
(a) Closed Undetected.
(b) Closed Detected.
(c) Cancelled.
(d) Prosecute.
(e) No Further Police Action.
In cases of Indictable Offences the final decision shall be
made by a First Division Officer.
In the case of a Summary Offence the final decision may be
given by an officer of the rank of Corporal and above.
(2) (a) “Closed Undetected” refers to instructions from an
officer where there is insufficient evidence to pros-
ecute anyone and such officer is satisfied that no
useful purpose will be served in continuing
enquiries.
(b)
“Closed Detected” refers to instructions given by
an officer who is satisfied that no useful purpose
will be served in prosecuting an offender although
a prima facie case has been made out.
(c) “Cancelled” refers to instructions from an officer
where he is satisfied that no offence has been
committed or where an offence has been commit-
ted and it has been wrongly classified.
