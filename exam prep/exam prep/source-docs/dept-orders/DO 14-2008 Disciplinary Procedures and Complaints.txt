PART 11
Disciplinary Procedure and Complaints
DISCIPLINARY PROCEDURE
AUTHORITY
1. Section 123A (1) of the Constitution of the Republic of Trinidad and Tobago, Chap 1:01 (as
amended) confers upon the Commissioner of Police the authority to exercise, among other things,
disciplinary control over the Police Service.
INTERPRETATION
2. In this Departmental Order-
“Alleged Defaulter” means a Police Officer who is alleged to have committed a breach of
discipline, or who is charged with a breach of discipline;
“Authority” means the Police Complaints Authority established under Section 5 of the Police
Complaints Authority Act No. 8 of 2006;
“Breach of Discipline” means a contravention of any of the provisions of the Police Service
Regulations 2007, or of any Police Service Order as defined in Standing Order No. 2 of
the Trinidad and Tobago Police Service Standing Orders 2001;
“Category “A” Offences” means those offences against discipline outlined in Appendix “A”,
and which shall in general be heard before a Tribunal consisting of three officers;
“Category “B” Offences” means those offences against discipline outlined in Appendix “B” and
which shall in general be heard before a Tribunal consisting of one officer:

“Tribunal” means an officer or a group of officers appointed by the Commissioner of Police to
hear and determine breaches of discipline, consisting of officers of the rank of
Superintendent or above, sitting as one officer, three officers, or such other uneven
number of officers as the Commissioner of Police may consider necessary;

Establishment Of Tribunals

3. (1) The Commissioner of Police may establish Tribunals at any Division/Branch/Section
consisting of such uneven number of officers as he considers necessary to conduct disciplinary
proceedings.

(2) Notwithstanding Paragraph 3 (1), Tribunals shall in general consist of either one officer or
three officers who shall hear and determine offences specifically designated to those Tribunals by
virtue of this Departmental Order.

(3) The Commissioner of Police shall appoint all Tribunals in writing.

(4) Where the Tribunal consists of more than one officer, the Commissioner of Police shall
appoint one of these officers as Chairperson.

4. Notwithstanding the classification of offences appearing at Appendix “A” and “B”, nothing in

Duties of the
Tribunal
5. (1) It shall be the duty of a Tribunal to, as far as is reasonably possible, hear the evidence, find the
facts and finally determine the matter within one month of the commencement of hearing into a
disciplinary matter.
(2) The Tribunal shall ensure that proceedings before it are held in private.
(3) At the conclusion of a matter, the Tribunal shall inform an Alleged Defaulter who is convicted
that he may:
a) appeal the decision of the Tribunal to the Police Service Commission; and
b) apply in writing to the Commissioner of Police for a copy of the record of the proceedings.
Power of a Tribunal
6. (1) A Tribunal shall have the power to dismiss a charge on any ground, or find the Alleged
Defaulter guilty of the charge and impose any one of the following penalties, namely:
a) Reduction in an office, that is, removal to another grade with an immediate reduction in
pay,
b) Reduction of remuneration, that is, an immediate adjustment of remuneration to a lower
point on the scale of remuneration attached to the particular office;
c) Deferment of increment, that is, a postponement of the date on which the next increment
is due, with corresponding postponements in subsequent years;
d) Stoppage of increment, that is, no payment for a specified period, of an increment
otherwise due;
e) Fine; or
f) Reprimand.

Rights of Alleged Defaulter
14, (1) An Alleged Defaulter has the right to:
a) the full opportunity to defend himself:
b) cite witnesses in his own defence, (he can request assistance from the Tribunal in this

d) either conduct his defence in person or be represented by another officer of his choice
or by his staff association or by an attorney-at-law, provided that where the Alleged
Defaulter appears before a Tribunal comprised of one officer, he shall not be represented
by an attorney-at-law;
€) cross examine witnesses called in support of the case against him;
f) make a submission at any time that the facts disclosed in the evidence do not support the
charge;
g) an appeal: and
h) a copy of the record of the proceedings.
Attire at Tribunals
15. Police Officers attending Tribunals must be attired in accordance with the instructions given at
Paragraphs 12 and 13 of Standing Order 6 “Attire and Appearance”.
Procedure at the hearing
Of the Tribunal
16. The procedure for hearing a Disciplinary Tribunal shall be as follows:

a) All evidence to be taken on oath;

b) The proceedings are to be recorded in writing and this record authenticated, at the
conclusion of each hearing by the Tribunal or Tribunal Chairperson, as the case may be,
affixing his/her signature and official stamp at the end of the record;

c) The prosecution witnesses are to be called first and the opportunity given to the defence
to cross examine these witnesses;

d) When a witness is cross-examined or re-examined, such evidence is to be recorded in the
form of questions and answers;

e) The standard of proof shall be on the “balance of probalities”:

f) After the close of the prosecution’s case, if there is a prima facie case made out against
the Alleged Defaulter, the Alleged Defaulter shall be called upon to answer the charge:

g) The prosecution shall be afforded the opportunity to cross-examine the Alleged Defaulter
or his witnesses after they have given their evidence-in-chief:

h) The Tribunal, after hearing the evidence of the prosecution, defence and all witnesses in
the matter would then be required to give its decision.

Hearing in the absence of the

Alleged Defaulter

17. Where an Alleged Defaulter has been properly served or warned in person by a Tribunal and does
not attend the hearing of the charge against him without good reason, the Tribunal may proceed with and
conclude the hearing in his absence, but if good reason is given to the Tribunal by or on behalf of the
Alleged Defaulter why he is unable to attend the hearing, the hearing shall be adjourned.

Hearing in the absence of the

Prosecutor

18. Where the prosecutor is absent without satisfactory explanation, the Tribunal shall adjourn the
matter and inform the officer in charge of the Division, Branch or Section where the prosecutor is posted
and that officer shall make enquiries and inform the Tribunal in writing before the next hearing why the
prosecutor was absent.

Procedure for Appeals

19. An Alleged Defaulter who is aggrieved by the decision of a Tribunal may file a Notice of Appeal in
writing with the Commission, in the form and manner prescribed by the Commission.
