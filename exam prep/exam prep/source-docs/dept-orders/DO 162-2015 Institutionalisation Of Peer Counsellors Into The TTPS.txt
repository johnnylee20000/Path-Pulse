INSTITUTIONALISATION OF PEER COUNSELLORS INTO THE TTPS
1. INFORMATION
1.1 The Police Service, in keeping with its strategic goal of strengthening the organization, has
trained two hundred and three (203) Second Division Officers, twenty-five (25) First
Division Officers and twenty six (26) Civilian employees from across the Trinidad and
Tobago Police Service (TTPS) in Peer Counselling.
12 Peer counselling training was geared towards expanding the support services available to
police officers and by extension civilian employees of the TTPS.
Trained officers and civilians are now equipped with the requisite skills and knowledge to
provide psycho-emotional support to their peers. They are now functioning as “Peer
Counsellors” by providing support to their peers in an informal setting, but which is guided
by a set of established ethical guidelines.
The training took place over the period October 13", 2014 to March 28, 2015 and
culminated with a Graduation on May 28, 2015.
2 INTENTION
2.1 The stressful nature of policing can sometimes have a negative effect on the mental and
physical health of officers; their family life and their ability to perform optimally and
effectively. Peer Counselling is being used as a tool to expand the support services
available to officers to minimize the negative impact of stress and to create an avenue
for individual healing and well-being of officers, and by extension to improve the
quality of service delivery by the officers.

3.1 Reports of Misconduct and/or Breaches of Confidentiality
A Committee consisting of five (5) members shall be established to hear and settle all complaints
including breaches of confidentiality or any type of misconduct made against a Peer Counsellor.
Composition of Committee:
1) Senior Superintendent Human Resource;
2) A representative from the Police Academy,
3) A representative from the Social Work Unit of the TTPS;
4) Two Peer Counsellors inclusive of a Police Officer and a Civilian staff member.
3.2 Ethics and Protocol Guidelines
Peer Counsellors were guided by the “TTPS Ethical and Protocol Guidelines” which outlines the
Code of Ethics and other Protocols by which he/she must abide. Failure to operate within the
parameters of the principles outlined in this guideline can result in the Peer Counsellor being
debarred from functioning as a Peer Counsellor and his/her name being removed from the list of
recognized TTPS Peer Counsellors. The TTPS Code of Ethics and Protocol Guidelines is
accessible to all employees of the TTPS via the TTPS Portal and website.
The objectives of viewing the Protocol is to:
“+ View the high standards to which Peer Counsellors are expected to abide/observe;

4, METHODOLOGY
Officers who expressed interested to function as peer counsellors were screened and interviewed
by aconsultant prior to being selected to participate in the programme. Those selected to be trained
as peer counsellors agreed to their participation and understood that the service they will be
providing is strictly voluntary and no monetary rewards will be received.
5. ADMINISTRATION
5.1 Administrative
The Senior Superintendent Human Resource Branch and the Director Human Resources
are responsible for establishing and maintaining the necessary structures to support the peer
counselling programme.
5.3 Monitoring and Evaluation of the Programme
The Research, Curriculum, Quality Assurance and Accreditation Unit of the Police .
Academy will conduct annual evaluation of the programme to ascertain its impact and
identify weaknesses in the programme.
6.0 INSTITUTIONALIZING OF PEER COUNSELLING INTO THE TTPS
The findings of an evaluation exercise of the Peer Counselling programme informed the decision
to institutionalize peer counselling by having the training form part of the training curriculum of
the Police Academy for both new recruits and enlisted police officers. The Police Academy is
responsible for ensuring that the peer counselling training programme becomes a permanent
feature on its training curriculum.
6.1 Role of the Police Academy
The role of the Police Academy is critical to the successful implementation of the programme.
Representatives from the Academy will form part of a Lead Body which will be charged with the
responsibilities of developing and implementing policies, ensuring the successful implementation
of the programme, work with the National Training Agency (NTA) to develop “Standards” to
govern the programme.

6.2 Train-the-Trainer Component
In preparation of the institutionalization of the programme into the TTPS, thirty Nine (39) Peer
Counsellors participated in a Train-the-Trainer module. Further, they participated in an Instructor
Development (ID) training programme facilitated by the NTA to ensure they possess the requisite
skills and techniques to teach the programme at the Police Academy. This ID training is critical
aspect of the accreditation possess for the Police Academy.
These 39 persons will be required from time-to-time to deliver the programme to new recruits at
the Police Academy. Whenever they are required the Police Academy will inform the Deputy
Commissioner “Administration’ via official correspondence. The Deputy Commissioner
Administration will be responsible for making the necessary arrangements to have the officers
identified to deliver the programme temporarily assigned to the Police Academy until the
completion of the training period, which should not exceed five (5) working days.
Further, these certified trainers will also be expected to deliver training of police officers
throughout the TTPS, over time.

DEFINITIONS
Definitions of key words
Confidential Space — any physical space as agreed by the peer counsellor and his peer to conduct
a peer counselling session
Peer/Client - refers to a colleague/employee of TTPS who is seeking the services of the peer
counsellor.
Peer Counsellor - refers to a person who is trained to effectively work with their peer to help them
explore and understand problems, and consider strategies to bring about personal change.
Peer Counselling — refers to the process by which an individual gives non-judgmental,
nondirective support to a peer who is experiencing a personal crisis. It is short term and provided
by a lay person rather than a professional therapist.
