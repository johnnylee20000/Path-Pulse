PART II
Trinidad and Tobago Police Service
Trauma Kit/Bandage Experiment

L PURPOSE - The purpose of this Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is to
define guidelines governing the use of the Trauma Plate Pack/Haemostatic
Bandage.

Il. BENEFITS - Some of the intended benefits of the SOP include the following:
a. Reduce the risk of death to police officers shot in the line of duty
b. Save lives of wounded persons
c. Reduce the homicide rate
d. Detect more homicides with victim testimony
e. Emphasize the role of TTPS as caring, courageous heroes who save lives.

Ill | SCOPE - This procedure applies to all Inter-Agency Task Force, Divisional
Task Force and Rapid Response Unit Officers who will be using the Trauma
Plate Pack, and any other TTPS officer who may have occasion to use trauma
kits on injured persons.

IV. REQUIREMENT AND MISSION - As a first responder, you will be
required to render medical aid to traumatic wound victims. Blood loss is the
primary cause of death in traumatic wound victims. Reducing blood loss is a
major opportunity for TTPS to save lives.

V. TRAINING - Many but not all officers have been sensitized and trained on
how to treat with, and use a trauma kit, in situations where a police officer
may have rendered aid to someone found suffering from a gunshot or knife
wound. Untrained officers are subject to this SOP as well as trained officers,
but should take guidance from any trained officer who may be present at the
scene.

VL + NON-MEDICAL STATUS - Use of a trauma kit is not a medical procedure.
It employs a first aid response geared to stop excessive bleeding and possibly
save a life. Just as a parent may render first aid to a child, or a neighbor may
place a bandage on a neighbor’s wound, police officers are allowed to do this
with all injured persons. Moreover, this SOP requires that police officers use
these trauma kits whenever a need and opportunity arises.

VII. EQUIPMENT - Each vehicle in the targeted units will receive a numbered
TRAUMA KIT that will remain in the vehicle. The numbered TRAUMA KIT
will contain:

a. 3 “TRAUMA PLATE” PACKS that each contain the HAEMOSTATIC
BANDAGE and GAUZE

b. 2 sets of gloves

c. 2 sets of masks

d. 3 “EMERGENCY BLANKET” WRAPPED PACKAGES. One of these is
to be placed in the vehicle before transporting a victim.

e. 1 pair of scissors

IX. PROCEDURE. The ten-point procedure officers should take when

using a trauma kit is as follows:

1) OPEN THE “TRAUMA PLATE” PACK

2) PUT ON GLOVES (mandatory) AND MASK (optional)

3) REMOVE EXCESS POOLED BLOOD FROM THE WOUND WITH THE
GAUZE

4) PLACE THE HAEMOSTATIC BANDAGE TIGHTLY AND DIRECTLY
ONTO THE BLEEDING SOURCE.

5) QUICKLY APPLY PRESSURE. This pressure can continue to be applied
while transporting the victim to the nearest medical facility.

6) LEAVE THE HAEMOSTATIC BANDAGE IN PLACE AND WRAP THE
WOUND WITH GAUZE TO SECURE THE BANDAGE IN PLACE

7) OPEN EMERGENCY BLANKET PACKAGE AND PLACE FULLY
OPENED BLANKET ON FLOOR OF VEHICLE

8) PLACE WOUNDED PERSON ON BLANKET IN VEHICLE

9) TRANSPORT WOUNDED PERSON TO THE NEAREST MEDICAL
FACILITY.

10) KEEP EMPTY TRAUMA PLATE PACK PACKAGE AND PROVIDE TO
EMERGENCY PERSONNEL AT THE MEDICAL FACILITY.

X. REPORTING. The three reporting requirements for each and every use
of a trauma kit are as follows:
a) CALL BANDAGE HOTLINE within 24 hours of the incident: 552.5 253
b) LEAVE A RECORDING WITH THE FOLLOWING DETAILS:
1. NAME, REGIMENTAL NUMBER
2. DATE, TIME, LOCATION OF INCIDENT
3. STATION/DIVISION/UNIT
4. KIT NUMBER OF TRAUMA KIT DEPLOYED
5. BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF INCIDENT (e.g. a civilian has been shot by
another civilian, an officer has been shot by a civilian, a civilian has been shot
by an officer, et cetera)
6. NUMBER OF BANDAGES USED. Any items used will be replaced with new
items returned to the kit number deployed within 24 hours.
c) File full report on the incident according to normal procedures and protocols for
investigations and report writing on incidents of that type.
XI. DEFINITIONS:
HAEMOSTATIC AGENT: A sterile, traumatic wound treatment that rapidly
stops high-volume blood loss. It affects coagulation in moderate-to-severe
wounds.
HAEMORRHAGE: high-volume blood loss
COAGULATION: Clotting of blood.
GAUZE WRAP: fabric used to clot blood and wrap around bandage
GENERAL INFORMATION:
It is to be noted that this experiment is in keeping with Strategic Goal #1 of the TTPS
Strategic Plan 2014 — 2016 i.e. “To Reduce and Detect Crime” , Strategic Objective #1.4
ie. ‘To Establish an Evidence-Based Policing Culture’ and Strategic Action #1.4.6 i.e.
‘To Conduct Experiments and Quasi Experiments on Specially Selected Policing and
Crime Control Strategies’.
