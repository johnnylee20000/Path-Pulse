PART II
FIREARM OFFENCES AS SERIOUS CRIMES
BACKGROUND Within the last decade Trinidad and Tobago has seen the prevalence of
firearm-related offences inclusive of firearm-related Murders. Whilst the
penalties for these offences increased on two occasions since 1995 by virtue
of amendments to Section 6 of the Firearm Act, Chapter 16:01 through Act
22 of 1995 and later on Act 2 of 2011, there has not been a concomitant
decrease in these offences. In 1995, the penalty for the offence of Possession
of a Firearm on summary conviction changed from a fine of 4,000 and
imprisonment for 2 years to a fine of 10,000 and imprisonment for 5 years.
This penalty, was again amended in 2011 to a fine of 15,000 and
imprisonment for 8 years. On the other hand, the penalty for the offence of
Possession of Firearm on indictment changed in 1995, from imprisonment
for 5 years to imprisonment for 10 years. This was later amended in 2011
to imprisonment for 15 years.
NATURE AND The prevalence with which crimes are committed via the use of firearms,
PURPOSE the increasing number of firearms found, recovered and seized remains a
concern for not only the Trinidad and Tobago Police Service (TTPS) but
also, the public at large. It is no secret that firearms can be destructive
weapons and its illegal (or legal) use can cause tremendous physical,
emotional, psychological and social harm, even death. It is for the reasons
outlined above and the fact that by virtue of the amendment to the Firearm
Act, Chapter 16:01, Section 6, the offence of possession of a firearm can
now be classified as a Serious Crime and will adopt the classification code
of 67. Therefore, with effect from 1st January, 2017, all firearm and firearm-
related offences would be classified as Serious Crimes.
