Glossary of Terms 
Governance - refers to the system, structure and/or practices that an organisation establishes to 
ensure that policies, plans, programs, a nd projects relative to its strategic direction order are being 
implemented. Governance is also concerned with overseeing the delivery of services, monitoring 
and mitigating key risks, reporting on performance in achieving intended results and using 
performance information to drive ongoing improvements and corrective actions.  
Oversight - is the tool or process that is used to ensure that a strategic plan is achieving the 
implementation of expected policies and strategies as intended. Additionally, oversigh t monitors 
that the strategic outcomes comply with required policies, laws, regulations and ethical standards.  
Major accomplishment - Major accomplishments refers to those significant achievements of the 
TTPS which is indicative of extreme effort, skill and dedication based on organisational goals, 
values and context of the accomplishment.  
Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design – Crime Prevention Through Environmental 
Design (CPTED) is “a multi -disciplinary approach to crime prevention that uses urban and 
architectural design and the management of built and natural environments” (NSW, 2024). A 
CPTED project focuses on Territoriality, Surveillance and Access Control and is a written plan  
developed and intended to reduce crime by changing the design of a building’s physical 
infrastructure, changing the design of streets, parks and other public spaces to reduce crime 
opportunities.  
Focused deterrence  – strategies that seek to change offender behaviour by understanding the 
underlying dynamics and implementing a blended strategy involving specific sanctions (or 
“levers”), as well as clear incentives for not engaging in crime. 
Precision Policing – According to Bratton and Murad (2018), precision policing is “a framework 
to ensure that the police collaborate with the community in meaningful ways – building police 
legitimacy because the methods are integrated into the heart of patrol wor k, not viewed as an 
ancillary function. Precision policing ensures that police use connectivity more than enforcement 
and that when enforcement is necessary, it is accurately focused”. Thus, precision policing allows 
us to “make every police-citizen interaction an act of collaboration”.

Precision Patrol – Precision Patrol is a patrol strategy focused on a specific geographic area to 
prevent crime but more importantly to build the relationship between the police and communities in 
the specific geographic a rea. Thus, it involves significant communication with members of those 
communities to build trust and confidence whilst patrolling areas where legitimacy might be low 
and which are prone to criminal and deviant conduct.

1.0 Introduction 
1.1 Background 
According to Cheung and Yu (2020), “strategic planning aims to shift traditional focus on existing 
challenges towards the exploration and understanding of possible futures”. Police strategic planning 
has changed from a traditional reactive-orientated policing to focus more on proactive policing with 
the intention of responding to the requirement for adjustment to our changing operating environment 
(Zhao et al., 2008). A Strategic Plan is therefore a pro-active tool that provides the guidance to an 
organization on how to attain its vision and fulfil its mission with maximum efficiency and impact.   
This Trinidad and Tobago Police Service (TTPS) Strategic Plan spans the period 2025 to 2027 and 
charts the bold course for evolution and excellence in policing which is aligned to the principles of 
an effective law enforcement system as outlined in our National Development Plan . It is grounded 
in our mission to provide safe and secure communities and other places in collaboration with our 
stakeholders through professional policing, focused leadership and consistent high quality service. 
The plan also reflects  our vision to make every place in Trinidad and Tobago safe, as well as our 
steadfast commitment to protecting the communities we serve, improving police legitimacy, 
nurturing trust and confidence and driving innovation throughout the organisation.  
This Strategic Plan will be used by internal stakeholders who will be aware of and work towards 
achieving our targets. Our external stakeholders will also utilize this Plan and will become au 
courant with the activities in which th e TTPS will engage and hold us accountable. This plan was 
formulated internally by the Trinidad and Tobago Police Service with the assistance and support of 
members of the Planning Research and Project Implementation Unit, the Monitoring Evaluation and 
Compliance Unit and the Executive Management Services Secretariat.  
The formulation of The Plan involved a comprehensive and systematic process which drew upon a 
variety of inputs to ensure its effectiveness. Further, it was developed in alignment with the 
organizational goals, and responsiveness to the needs of the TTPS stakeholders. Key elements of 
The Plan include internal and external assessments, policies and legal guidelines, strategic inputs, 
benchmarking and best practices, identification of potential risks and threats, community and 
cultural considerations and monitoring and evaluation processes.  The integration of these elements 
provides for an actionable framework tailored to the unique needs , challenges and opportunities 
facing the TTPS and communities in Trinidad and Tobago.

1.2 Purpose 
The purpose of The  Plan i s to provide a clear, structured roadmap which guides the TTPS in 
achieving its goals, addressing challenges and delivering effective, efficient and equitable services 
to the public. To establish a clear sense of purpose and unified direction, the four strategic objectives 
for the TTPS over the period 2025 to 2027 will be highlighted and elaborated upon. It is integral for 
the TTPS to address the critical issues such as public safety and crime prevention and align the 
necessary resources to these issues if we are to be successful.  
Considering the TTPS does not function in a vacuum and understanding the importance of 
accountability and transparency, the strategic initiatives in this plan demonstrate the TTPS’ 
commitment to transparency by outlining priorities, key performance indicators, expected outcomes 
and targets .  These principles provide a basis for measuring the performance of the TTPS and 
holding the organization accountable to its many internal and external stakeholders.  
This plan is also aimed at improving service delivery. Therefore, The Plan guides the 
implementation of the selected initiatives which would assist in improving operational efficiency , 
officer training and the use of technology. It will ensure that policing strategies are responsive to the 
needs and expectations of the local communities and aligned with national expectations. As such, it 
is necessary that in The Plan, there is an integration of the police service’s objectives with broader 
national security and public safety strategie s. Similarly, emerging challenges such as crime trends 
which are evolving, technological advances and societal expectations will also be addressed in The 
Plan.  It is in this regard, Trinidad and Tobago ’s development strategies, the Strategic Plan of the 
Ministry of National Security and the Public Sector Investment Programme are some of the key 
documents and initiatives for such integration.   
In taking into account the Police Service Commission survey and other key research documents, 
there is the clear and unambiguous need for us to do more to build public trust and confidence. As 
such The Plan reinforces the TTPS’ commitment to serving with integrity and professionalism. It is

1.7 History of the Trinidad and Tobago Police Service 
Undoubtedly, the Trinidad and Tobago Police Service (TTPS) has a rich history which informs this 
Strategic Plan for the 2025 to 2027 period. Key historical milestones include:  
a. 1592 marked the early e stablishment of the Trinidad and Tobago Police Force . Founded 
under Spanish rule, the initial police force was limited to duties in the towns with a small 
team of officers.  
b. Post-Emancipation Expansion (1838): Following the abolition of slavery, the force expanded 
to rural areas, establishing multiple stations to serve the needs of a growing population.  
c. Organizational Reforms (1869): An ordinance improved the force's organization and 
discipline, enhancing surveillance and public order capabilities.

d. In 1955 women police were included as p art of the force: Recognizing the need to address

e. With the need to show a more humanitarian face and service orientation, the police force

f. 1970 to present, represents an era of modernization and growth : The TTPS has grown to a

These historical developments have shaped the TTPS' current structure and strategies, emphasizing

3.0 Situational Analysis 
3.1 Political Environment 
The Government of Trinidad and Tobago has made a clear commitment to tackling crime and 
enhancing the safety and security of the nation. This ongoing effort is evident in the support provided 
to the Trinidad and Tobago Police Service (TTPS) to improve its effectiveness and operational 
capacity. Key initiatives include increasing the number of police officers and ensuring the provision 
of the necessary tools, equipment, and technology needed for effective intelligence gathering and 
investigations. 
In July 2024, the Honorable Keith Scotland was appointed as a Minister within the Ministry of 
National Security, with a specific focus on overseeing the TTPS. His appointment was aimed at 
addressing the challenges faced by the police service, and ensuring that the necessary resources were 
provided to enhance its operational effectiveness.  Similarly, d uring the last  strategic period, 
significant strides were made in strengthening the leadership and governance of the police service. 
The Police Service Commission appointed a substantive Commissioner of Police, along with three 
(3) Deputy Commissioners of Police. This a ppointment has had a substantial positive impact, 
providing much-needed leadership stability to the operations of the TTPS. 
Through these concerted efforts, the Government of Trinidad and Tobago is demonstrating its 
determination to create a safer environment for its citizens while reinforcing the capabilities of the 
TTPS to address the evolving challenges of crime and law enforcement in the country. 
3.2 Socio-Economic Environment  
The energy sector continues to face several challenges which causes a ripple effect on the wider 
economy. Domestically, the decline in production levels is exacerbating an already volatile global 
energy market, leading to price fluctuations that can disrupt economic stability. This volatility has 
had a negative impact on government  revenues and foreign exchange reserves, further straining 
external liquidity. As foreign exchange reserves deplete, the foreign exchange mar ket is facing 
imbalances, which in turn, has a detrimental effect on the non -energy sector. The limited access to 
US dollars has created significant difficulties for businesses that rely on imports. This not only 
strains relationships with international partners but also slows down business operations, leading to 
an increase in costs that are eventually passed on to consumers. 



27 
 
While inflation has been on a downward trajectory which is a positive sign for purchasing power, 
rising food prices remain a persistent issue for citizens. This issue contributes to broader economic 
pressure and could potentially fuel social unrest. The strain on consumer spending, especially in the 
face of food insecurity, may create a fertile ground for criminal activity. Consequently, the TTPS 
faces heightened pressure as it grapples with both the increasing incidence of crime and a growing 
demand for resources, which are already stretched thin.  
3.3 Technological Environment 
The TTPS is increasingly lever aging technological advances  to improve its effectiveness in 
managing crime prevention and ensuring public safety. However, this comes with both opportunities 
and challenges, as technology can be used by criminals to bypass detection, while also providing 
law enforcement with the tools to better combat crime. 
The TTPS has acquired new technologies such as: 
 Body worn cameras  - these are b eing utilized by police officers to capture footage of 
interactions between officers and the public, offering an objective record of events. This can 
be critical in enhancing accountability, protecting both officers and civilians, and providing 
evidence in investigations. 
 Closed-circuit television (CCTV)  – these systems are used to monitor public spaces, 
providing real-time surveillance. This helps in c rime detection, deterring criminal activity, 
and assisting in post-crime investigations by providing valuable video evidence. 
 Dashboard Cameras – these cameras are mounted in police vehicles and are essential for 
documenting traffic stops, pursuits, and ot her police activities. They provide an additional 
layer of accountability, offering evidence in case of disputes. 
 Drones – drones allow the TTPS to monitor large areas from the air, providing enhanced 
surveillance during major public events, search and res cue operations, or crime scene 
investigations. Drones can access hard -to-reach areas, improving response times and 
operational efficiency. 
 E-ticketing systems  are helping law enforcement officers issue citations for traffic 
violations more efficiently. The se devices automate the process, making it easier to track 
offences and reduce paperwork. 



28 
 
 GPS Handsets technology enables real-time tracking of police officers and vehicles. This 
can improve response times to incidents and enhance coordination during operations. It also 
provides a way to monitor the whereabouts of officers for safety and efficiency. 
 Laser speed guns are used to detect speeding vehicles with greater accuracy than traditional 
radar guns. This technology enhances road safety by allowing officers to enforce speed limits 
more effectively. 
While the introduction of these technologies can significantly enhance the TTPS' capacity to prevent 
and respond to crime, it also presents challenges. Criminals are increasingly using sophisticated 
methods such as hacking, encryption, 3D printing, quantum computing and digital anonymity to 
commit crimes and evade detection. The TTPS must therefore continuously evolve and integrate 
new tools and strategies to stay ahead of these threats. 
As technology advances, the TTPS must balance the benefits of these tools with the ethical, legal, 
and privacy concerns that may arise. Adequate training, policies, and oversight will be key in 
ensuring that these technologies are used effectively to protect both public safety and  individual 
rights. The integration of Artificial Intelligence, machine learning, and other emerging technologies 
could further transform policing, enabling predictive policing and improving overall public safety 
management. 
3.4 Legal Environment 
The TTPS as the primary institution responsible for law enforcement in the country, faces a dynamic 
and ever -evolving legal environment. This environment necessitates that the TTPS stays well -
informed about the current laws and is adaptable to the demands imposed by ne wly introduced or 
revised legislation. Meeting these obligations can be particularly challenging, as the requirements 
often involve a broad range of adjustments. These may include modifications to standing orders, 
regulations and procedures, changes in the  assignment and training of personnel, as well as the 
provision of necessary physical infrastructure and other essential resources. Such comprehensive 
and ongoing changes require the TTPS to be agile and responsive to maintain its effectiveness in 
upholding the law. 
Recently the Administration of Justice Indictable Proceedings Act repealed and replaced the 
Indictable Offences (Preliminary Inquiry) Act, Chap. 12:01 wh ich eliminates the preliminary 
inquiries in indictable matters. The removal of Preliminary Inquiries and the introduction of a more 



29 
 
effective case management process allows for the criminal justice system to experience a reduction 
in the backlog of cases, fewer hearings and significantly less delays in proceedings.  
The Public Procurement and Dis posal of Public Property Act was fully proclaimed on April 26, 
2023, through Legal Notice No. 106 of 2023. In accordance with this legislation, the TTPS is 
required to comply with the provisions of this Act, which governs public procurement and the 
management, retention, and disposal of public property. The Act is founded on the core principles 
of good governance, accountability, integrity, transparency, and value for money. It also aims to 
encourage sustainable procurement practices, support sustainable de velopment, and foster the 
growth of local industry. Over the course of this three (3) year strategic period, the TTPS plans to 
acquire several major assets, all of which will be procured under the framework established by this 
legislation. 
3.5 Environmental 
Trinidad and Tobago grapples with high rates of violent crime, including homicides, robberies, and 
gang-related activities. This poses significant challenges to the TTPS in maintaining public safety 
and security. The Caribbean region serves as a major transit point for transnational criminal activities 
such as drug trafficking, human trafficking, arms smuggling, and money laundering.  
This critical issue is further compounded by the prevalence of illegal firearm s and drugs which 
increase gang-related activities within the country. The Caribbean region is plagued by transnational 
crime syndicates involved in drug trafficking, arms smuggling, human trafficking, and other illicit 
activities. Organized crime networks often operate across national borders, presenting a formidable 
challenge to law enforcement agencies. The porous nature of maritime borders in the Caribbean 
exacerbates these issues, making it difficult to intercept illegal shipments and apprehend criminal 
elements.

5.2 Justification for a Bottom-up Approach 
On this occasion, the TTPS decided to use a bottom -up approach in dev eloping the Strategic Plan 
for 2025 -2027 and the Operating Plans for 2025, 2026 and 2027. It was felt that a bottom -up 
approach could derive the following benefits:  
i. Enhanced relevance and practicality: -  
a. This will allow officers at different levels and ranks in the TTPS to contribute to the 
development of the plan, especially the most junior members of staff. The ground level 
officers have first -hand experience with the needs of communities, and know and 
understand their challenges and operational realit ies in providing for safe and secure 
communities and other places. Thus, incorporating grassroots perspectives can assist in 
ensuring the strategic plan effectively addresses real issues. 
b. A bottom-up approach allows input from various Divisions, Sections, Branches and Units, 
thus tailoring the action items, targets and strategies to the specific needs of diverse 
stakeholders across Trinidad and Tobago.  
 
ii. Increased buy-in and commitment to task:-  
a. Ownership of the Plan - The employees are more likely to take ownership of the plan and 
feel a sense of belonging and the need for accountability for its outcomes, when they have 
contributed to the planning process.  
b. Improved Morale  – With inclusion, a sense of value and respect amongst sworn and 
unsworn officers is  fostered. This will in turn boost morale and motivation in the 
implementation of the plan. 
 
iii. Better communication and collaboration:- 



37 
 
a. Bridging Gaps - Through the bottom -up approach, we encouraged dialogue within the 
Executive, amongst other First Division Officers and between Civilian Staff and Second 
Division Officers. Thus, obtaining the perspectives of personnel at all levels sought to 
promote greater understanding and collaboration in an attempt to minimize possible 
disconnects and avoid the Ivory Tower Syndrome, where managers can lose touch with 
the beat of the organization and the communities they serve.  
b. Breaking Hierarchical Barriers - A bottom-up approach fosters a culture of inclusivity, 
which breaks down silos within the TTPS.  
 
iv. Adaptability and Innovation:-  
a. Diverse Perspectives – It was assumed that with a bottom -up approach the TTPS would 
be able to obtain the views from a broad range of civilian and police personnel at various 
Divisions and Departments. These officers represented different ranks , positions, ages, 
gender, ethnicities, religious persuasions and communities in which they reside. 
Therefore, it was felt that the diverse backgrounds can lead to more innovative solutions 
which a top-down approach may have overlooked.   
b. Flexibility – The TTPS felt that grassroots input would ensure that the plan remains 
adaptable to the dynamic challenges, for example, evolving crime trends or community 
concerns or rapid changes in technology.  
 
v. Alignment with Community-Oriented Policing Goals:- 
a. Strengthening Community Trust – It was thought that a bottom -up approach is in 
alignment with community -oriented policing principles by integrating insights from 
officers who interact directly with the public.

5.6 Limitations and Delimitations  
Overview 
As the TTPS moves forward with the 2025 –2027 Strategic Plan, the organization reflects on the 
achievements and challenges of the previous period, recognizing areas that were succe ssful and 
identifying those that require further attention. In doing so, the TTPS acknowledges the external and 
internal limitations and delimitations that influenced the 2022 -2024 plan. These factors, such as 
political shifts, budget constraints, and societal changes—including an aging population, increased 
immigration, and evolving crime patterns—will continue to shape the strategies and priorities of the 
TTPS in the coming years. 
The political climate, particularly in the lead -up to the 2025 general elections, remains a key 
consideration. Political decisions, including budget allocations and policy changes, will impact the 
TTPS’s ability to implement its crime -fighting initiatives.  The TTPS’ operations will also be 
influenced by the economy, society, technology, legal issues and the environment. The TTPS must 
navigate these external pressures while remaining focused on its core mission: to make Trinidad and 
Tobago a safe and secure environment for all its citizens, residents and visitors. 
Limitations 
These limitations —including resource constraints, funding issues, bureaucratic challenges, 
represent significant hurdles for the TTPS in achieving the objectives outlined in the 2025 -2027 
Strategic Plan. However, by reco gnizing these limitations early, strategies can be developed to 
mitigate their impact, and the TTPS will be better able to navigate these challenges and continue to 
evolve as an effective law enforcement agency in the year s ahead. The limitations with this Plan 
include the following:  
 Crime Focus – Given our current circumstances and what is predicted, the plan has targeted 
serious criminal offending, with a focus on firearms, gang and drug-related matters. The Plan 
therefore does not address every crime category equally as some crime activities were 
considered to be of lower priority.   
 Geographical Challenges  – Some remote and high -crime areas are difficult to patrol 
effectively due to terrain or lack of infrastructure. 



42 
 
 Availability of Financial Resources – The quantum of the allocation to TTPS in the coming 
fiscal years, as well as the timeliness of disbursements received will impact all aspects of 
implementation. 
 System Integration Issues – The lack of interoperability between police databases and other 
government systems can hinder the TTPS’ efficiency. 
 Political and Administrative Boundaries – The Plan must align with national security 
policies which are set by the GO RTT and may be restricted by political will, changes i n 
government leadership, and timely budgetary allocations which are controlled by the 
Ministry of Finance.  
 International Law and Treaties – The TTPS is bound by international agreements, treaties 
and laws which can limit our ability to implement some strategies. 
 Legal and Judicial System Constraints – Policing efforts are largely dependent on other 
aspects of the criminal justice system. Thus limitations such as court backlog, inefficiency 
in prosecuting matters and even outdated laws may hinder enforcement, prevention and 
deterrence efforts. 
 Environmental and Disaster-Related Factors – The Plan does not directly cover policing 
efforts during unscheduled events, such as earthquakes, tsunamis, pandemics and hurricanes, 
for example and if any of these events should occur it could disrupt police operations.  
 Cultural and Social Considerations – The Plan aims to address crime on many fronts and 
includes social activities, but cultural barriers (such as low levels of police legitimacy / 
distrust of police, the influence of gangs in targeted communities) may limit our 
effectiveness with some of these initiatives.  
 Public perception and resistance – Some proposed initiatives may face resistance from 
members of the public in targeted communities and civil rights / human rights groups and 
this can adversely affect full implementation of The Plan.  
 International Criminal Networks – Drug trafficking and gun smuggling involve 
international actors, which limits TTPS’ ability to combat them independently, thus 
involving external stakeholders who may not share the same interest can compromise  the 
TTPS’ operations. 
 Technological Implementation Limits – Whilst The Plan proposes to further modernise 
the TTPS through technology, it is limited by TTPS’ technological infrastructure, 
cybersecurity risks and budgetary constraints.   



43 
 
 Human Resource Con straints – The TTPS does not currently have all the required 
personnel including specialised support to implement all aspects of The Plan fully.  
 Data and Intelligence Gaps – For crime prevention and detection to be effective, accurate 
crime data and intel ligence sharing is required. However, limitations in data collection, 
accuracy in reporting, and perhaps an outdated Records Management System (RMS) could 
compromise the full realization of our strategic objectives. 
Delimitations 
In the preparation of this  Trinidad and Tobago Police Service Strategic Plan 2025-2027 the 
following delimitations arose:  
 Time Frame – The Plan covers a period 2025-2027, so it does not factor long-term policing 
strategies which go beyond the 3-year period.  
 Geographic Scope – The Plan focuses primarily on Trinidad and Tobago and excludes in 
the main regional or international policing collaborations. 
 Jurisdictional Boundaries – The TTPS operates within its legal mandate related to public 
safety. However, issues which relate to defence are handled essentially by the Trinidad and 
Tobago Defence Force or other international bodies, such as Interpol and were therefore not 
included.   
 Policy and Legislative Constraints – The Plan is aligned to the current Laws of Trinidad 
and Tobago and policies of the Government of Trinidad and Tobago (GO RTT). Therefore, 
recommendations or initiatives which require constitutional/legislative amendments were 
not included but will be proposed otherwise.  
 Community Involvement Scope – Whilst the ideal vision of community policing is key for 
what the TTPS would like to achieve, the extent of public engagement is limited given 
available resources.   
 Inter-Agency Cooperation Limits  – Whilst the TTPS usually work s with other agencies 
and departments, such as the Customs and Excise Division, the Immigration Division, the 
Licensing Authority, the Trinidad and Tobago Fire Service and others, cooperation is 
restricted by bureaucratic inefficiencies, legal boundaries and lack of full and complete inter-
agency data sharing.   



44 
 
 Training and Development Restrictions – Although The Plan includes capacity building 
initiatives for officers - training resources, certified instructors or application of international 
best practice may be limited due to financial or logistical constraints.  
Whilst the TTPS’ Strategic Plan 2025-2027 outlines a comprehensive fra mework for improving 
policing throughout Trinidad and Tobago , the above limitations highlight the constraints within 
which the plan will operate. Factors such as the limitations with resources, legislative restrictions, 
the challenges with technology and infrastructure and even the socio -economic realities which we 
face may adversely affect the full implementation of these robust and ambitious strategic initiatives. 
However, as the TTPS clearly acknowledges these boundaries, adaptive strategies will be developed 
to address them. Further critical areas will be prioritised as we seek innovative solutions that would 
serve to overcom e these constraints. Consequently, as we move forward , continuous evaluation, 
inter-agency collaboration and stakeholder engagement will be essential in mitigating these 
delimitations and ensuring the successful execution of The Plan.

6.1 Purpose of Stakeholder Analysis 
A stakeholder analysis is an essential step in developing a strategic plan for the police service. This 
process enables the Trinidad and Tobago Police Service (TTPS) to identify individuals and groups 
with a vested interes t in its operations, understand their needs, and incorporate their perspectives 
into the planning process. Hereunder is provided, a detailed list of stakeholders who are directly or 
indirectly affected by, or can influence, the TTPS’ operations. This includes an assessment of their 
levels of influence, interest, impact, and expectations. 
By thoroughly analysing and understanding its stakeholders, the TTPS can achieve the following: 
 Foster Collaboration : Engaging key stakeholders builds strong partnerships, which are 
crucial for effective policing and for co-creating solutions to public safety challenges. 
 Enhance Transparency: Open communication of goals, strategies, and progress fosters trust 
among stakeholders, including citizens, international organizations, and internal personnel. 
 Promote Mutual Understanding : Addressing diverse stakeholder priorities —ranging from 
community safety to resource allocation for officers —ensures alignment with societal 
expectations and organizational objectives. 
 Improve Policy Implementation: Identifying potential areas of resistance or support allows 
the TTPS to design communication and engagement strategies that secure stakeholder buy -
in and mitigate implementation risks. 
 Support Informed Decision -Making: Considering stakehold ers' perspectives enables the 
TTPS to anticipate conflicts, address concerns, and align strategic goals with broader societal 
needs. 
By leveraging stakeholder analysis, the TTPS can create a more inclusive, transparent, and effective 
strategic plan, strengthening its ability to serve and protect the community.

stakeholders to address public safety issues, to solve problems and to improve the overall quality of

prevention and public safety. Community partnerships highlights the need for mutual trust, respect 
and the responsibility for the creation of safe and healthy communities which must be shared 
between the TTPS and all communities. In light of this, the TTPS will prioritize building trus t and 
fostering partnerships with the public. This collaboration is intended to ensure a safer environment 
for all citizens, encouraging active participation in crime prevention efforts and strengthening the 
bond between the police and the communities they serve.

investments will be critical in ensuring that the TTPS remains responsive to the evolving needs of 
society and continues to provide effective service to all citizens, irrespective of their background or 
demographic characteristics. 
The TTPS will place a strong emphasis on community policing as a cornerstone of its strategy to 
ensure public safety. This approach fosters collaboration and encourages citizens to play an active 
role in maintaining order and preventing crime in their communities.  The TTPS will organize 
initiatives such as Station Council meetings, community walkabouts, and focused patrols to engage 
directly with local resident s and address their concerns. These interactions will help build trust 
between the police service and the public, creating a safer environment where crime prevention 
becomes a shared responsibility. However, The TTPS understands that tackling crime require s 
collaboration beyond the police service. Therefore, it will work closely with a range of other 
stakeholders, including the judiciary, government agencies, and non -governmental organizations 
(NGOs), to provide a holistic approach to crime prevention. These collaborations will help create a 
comprehensive framework for addressing crime at all levels —from prevention and intervention to 
legal proceedings and rehabilitation.  Stakeholder engagement is also critical to the succ ess of the 
TTPS’ crime reduction strategy. By coordinating with other agencies, the TTPS will ensure that its 
efforts are aligned with broader national goals and policies. These collaborations will also enhance 
the ability of the TTPS to secure funding from various sources, including governm ent grants and 
international partnerships. 
Additionally, the TTPS will continue to uphold professionalism, ensuring that all public reports are 
handled through its one -door policy, a process that guarantees efficiency and transparency.  The 
success of community policing will also depend on budgetary considerations, as resources will be 
needed to support outreach activities, station council meetings, and community engagement efforts. 
It will be important for the TTPS to secure sufficient funding for these initiatives while maintaining 
its core operations.

8.3 Operational Excellence 
Strategic Intent - Operational Excellence is a key strategic pillar for the Trinidad and Tobago Police 
Service (TTPS). This focuses on improving the efficiency, effectiveness and overall performance of 
the TTPS. This strategic pillar is developed to enhance the TTPS’ ability to improve service delivery 
to our internal and external public. The action items outlined under this pillar will allow the TTPS 
to achieve operational excellence by building a more responsive, efficient and professional police 
service. The TTPS aims to s ignificantly reduce serious crimes, particularly murders and firearms -
related offences, by leveraging advanced technology. The adoption of cutting -edge tools, such as 
data analytics, predictive policing, and surveillance systems, will continue to enable the TTPS to 
identify crime hotspots, track criminal activity in real-time, and make more informed decisions about 
resource allocation.  
The success of this crime reduction strategy is expected to boost the legitimacy and effectiveness of 
the TTPS. However, this will require adequate funding and resource allocation. The TTPS will need 
to secure budgetary support for the procurement and maintenance of advanced technology, as well 
as for training officers to use these tools effectively. Budgetary constraints may also require careful 
prioritization of resources to ensure that technological advancements are integrated to improve 
service delivery without compromising other areas of policing.  



58 
Technological innovations are rapidly transforming law enforcement practices, and the TTPS aims 
to stay ahead of these trends. Key investments include:  
a)  Artificial Intelligence Software:  Automating data entry and report generation to increase 
administrative and operational efficiency. This allows officers to focus more on critical duties.  
b) Body-Worn Cameras (BWC):  These devices will enhance accountability and  improve 
evidence collection during police interactions. 
c) Facial Recognition Technology: This will help identify suspects in real time or from video 
footage. A possible integration with the Licensing Office could link vehicle license plates to 
their owners, further enhancing this capability.  
d) Drones for Aerial Surveillance:  Drones will assist in monitoring and gathering crucial 
information from the skies, supporting various aspects of crime prevention and investigation.  
e) DNA Databases: These databases will facilitate the identification of suspects and link them 
to crimes more efficiently.  
f) Predictive Policing Software: By analysing crime patterns, this software can predict potential 
crime hotspots, enabling proactive law enforcement efforts.  
g) Social Media Monitoring Tools: These tools will help track online threats and prevent harm 
to the public by monitoring conversations that may indicate potential risks.

9.1 Governance and Oversight  
The role of governance of The Plan has been assigned to the executive a rm of the TTPS – that is, 
the Commissioner of Police , Deputy Commissioners, Assistant Commissioners  of Police and 
Civilian Heads. These individuals have been given executive responsibility in the case of the 
Commissioner and Deputy Commissioners and functional responsibility in the case of the Assistant 
Commissioners and Civilian Heads, for the implementation of the Plan, and will be held accountable 
for the plan’s success or failure. Such responsibility is supported by the Constitution of the Republic 
of Trinidad and Tobago and the Police Service Act and Regulations, 2007. Whilst the outcome of 
The Plan hinges on the stewardship of the  Executive, the Police Internal Oversight Committee 
(PIOC) will also have oversight of The Plan (Appendix 6 refers).   
9.2 Stakeholder Engagement  
As the TTPS  strategic planning process unfolds, consistent stakeholder engagement and 
participation are paramount to the successful implementation of the Strategy. Th is emphasis is 
aligned with the adoption of a bottom -up approach, which fosters greater buy -in by providing 
stakeholders with opportunities to influence decisions. This approach represents a significant 
departure from traditional practices. Consequently, the TTPS’s leadership is committed to continue 
prioritizing stakeholder engagement to ensure alignment and commitment throughout the strategy’s 
implementation. 
Effective stakeholder engagement involves identifying, mapping, and prioritizing stakeholders to 
determine the most effective tactics for communication and collaboration during the strategic 
planning process.

9.8 Risk Management 
In this Strategic Plan, risk management is a critical component where unique challenges such as 
natural disasters, cross-border crime, and socio-economic issues often arise. Therefore, these risks 
will be managed through a systematic process which allows us to identify, assess, mitigate and 
monitor potential obstacles which may arise but which will hinder the achievement of the TTPS’ 
organisational goals.   
Understanding the Strategic Context 
Before incorporating risk management, it is essential to define the context for our strategic goals as 
discussed in the sections above, which cover: 
 Community Partnerships  
 Organisational Development  
 Operational Excellence & 
 Public Safety  
To mitigate against risks there must be an engagement of stakeholders, including all ranks and levels 
of officers and community  representatives, to identify priorities. It is therefore imperative for our 
strategic plan to be aligned with the wider national objectives and international standards. 
Identification of Risks 
Risks can stem from internal and external factors that may impact success of The Plan, such as: 
 Natural Disasters: Hurricanes, floods, and earthquakes can disrupt operations. 
 Crime Trends: Gang violence, drug trafficking, and cybercrime. 
 Resource Limitations: Budget constraints, outdated equipment, or insufficient staffing. 
 Public Relations: Loss of public trust due to misconduct or perceived inefficiency. 



73 
 Legislative and Policy Changes: New laws or political instability. 
Therefore, the TTPS acknowledges that its operations are influenced by many factors. As such, the 
action taken in The Plan includes the conduct of a risk assessment using a SWOT analysis to identify 
Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats and a PESTLE analysis looking at the political, 
economic, social, technological, legal and environmental conditions.  Notably, the year 2025 is an 
election year in Trinidad and Tobago, which could introduce political volatility and potential shifts 
in public policy. The TTPS recognizes that the gov ernment’s priorities and budgetary allocations 
during this period will directly impact its ability to execute crime -fighting initiatives. Changes in 
political leadership or shifts in public opinion may also affect the implementation of security policies 
or the public's trust in law enforcement. 
Evaluate and Prioritize Risks 
We also recognise that not all risks are equal. We will determine which risk poses the greatest threat 
to our strategic objectives by assessing their: 
 Likelihood: Probability of occurrence; and 
 Impact: Potential consequences for operations, reputation, or finances. 
The TTPS will use a risk matrix to classify risks as low, medium, high, or critical and we will focus 
our resources on addressing high and critical risks. 
Mitigation Strategies 
For each identified risk, we have developed strategies to minimize its likelihood or impact. Our 
mitigation measures are realistic and tailored to our context.  Some of our mitigation strategies will 
involve for example, in the case of a natural disaster; disaster response protocols; the  conduct of 
regular drills, and the establishment of  partnerships w ith regional emergency agencies such as 
CDEMA. 
Mitigation strategies based on crime trends will result in i ncreased intelligence sharing with 
neighbouring countries, investment in technology like CCTV and data analytics, and deployment of 
specialized units. Similarly, with resource limitations, the TTPS will advocate for budget increases 
and improved efficiency through training. Public relations will be critical, thus, community outreach 
programs will be fundamental and our campaign must enhance transparency.  Additionally, to allow 
for feedback, a system to encourage an independent complaint mechanism will be adopted. Further, 



74 
legislative changes may also be possible, so this would require all TTPS staff to be proactive, stay 
informed about upcoming laws and actively participate in policy discussions. 
Implement Risk Monitoring and Response Plans 
The TTPS will i ntegrate risk manag ement into our day-to-day operations to ensure continuous 
monitoring and adaptability. Therefore, the action to be taken will involve the assignment of a risk 
management officer or team to oversee implementation of the Risk Monitoring and Response Plan. 
Thus, key performance indicators (KPIs) will be established to measure progress and contingency 
plans for high -priority risks, detailing specific actions, responsibilities, and communication 
protocols will be created. 
Review and Adjust Regularly 
In recognition of the fact that r isk management is an ongoing process, p eriodic reviews will allow 
us to adapt to evolving challenges and opportunities.  Therefore, quarterly or semi -annual ris k 
reviews will be adopted and after-action reports will be prepared following major inciden ts to 
identify lessons learnt. Consequently, The Plan will be updated as and when necessary to reflect 
changes in the risk landscape. 
A fundamental objective is to reduce gang-related violence.  Consequently, there could be the risk 
of retaliatory violence from gangs which could escalate tensions and disrupt progress of The Plan. 
In such circumstances, to mitigate against such a risk, it may become necessary to implement a 
targeted gang intervention programme or collaborate with community leaders to mediate conflicts. 
As such, intelligence capabilities which can anticipate and prevent retaliation will be strengthened. 
Effective risk management is essential for the success of The Plan. By identifying, evaluating, and 
addressing risks proactively, we can enhance our resilience and achieve  our long-term objectives. 
This approach not only ensures operational success but also strengthens public trust and safety 
within the national community.

10.0 Conclusion and Call to Action 
10.1 Conclusion 
The 2025-2027 Strategic Plan of the TTPS represents a bold but transformative roadmap toward a 
safer nation and more resilient police service. The Plan is rooted in the four pillars of Community 
Partnerships, Organisational Develo pment, Operational Excellence and Public Safety . Thus, this 
plan is designed to modernise policing and restore public trust. Over the next three years the TTPS 
will take daring and decisive action to strengthen our operational effectiveness, we will levera ge 
cutting-edge technology and deepen partnerships with communities. It is through data -driven 
decision-making, proactive management and crime prevention, looking after the health and welfare 
of our most valuable resource – our human resource and the enhancement of officer training that we 
will work extremely hard to ensure that every citizen feels safe in their home, school, workplace and 
community.  
Whilst we will always have challenges, The Plan outlines clear strategies to address violent crime, 
improve road safety, combat corruption and enhance service delivery. It is through strong leadership, 
integrity and collaboration with our internal and external stakeholders, the TTPS  is committed to 
upholding its mission in protecting and serving its citizens wi th professionalism, respect, integrity, 
dignity and excellence. 
10.2 Call to Action 
The success of this strategic plan is closely tied to the dedication, well -being, and efficiency of its 
workforce and depends not only on the TTPS but also on collaborative effo rts between other arms 
of law enforcement, government agencies, businesses, community leaders and all Trinidad and 
Tobago citizens. Therefore, we call on:  
i. TTPS police officers and civilian staff to embrace The Plan with dedication, humility, ethics, 
continuously improving your skills and engaging the public.  
ii. Community members and civil society to actively participate in crime prevention initiatives, 
neighbourhood watch programs, station councils and youth development efforts.  
iii. The private sector and international partners to invest in security innovations and contribute 
to sustainable solutions such as the Eagle Eye intended for crime reduction.  
The vision of making every place in Trinidad and Tobago safe, will also result in improved security 
and prosperity in Trinidad and Tobago and these ideals are within reach. So, by uniting efforts, 



77 
embracing innovation and fostering trust, we can build a police service that is not only effective, 
efficient and accountable but also truly reflective of the values and aspirations of the people whom 
we continuously serve.  In closing, we can all agree that there can be no other time, now is the time 
to act! Together, we can create a future where every citizen feels protected, justice prevails and the 
Trinidad and Tobago Police Service stands as a beacon of excellence, hope and resilience.

Appendix 6: PIOC Brief 
Police Internal Oversight Committee (PIOC) 
The Police Internal Oversight Committee (PIOC) was established  by the Commissioner of Police 
(CoP) in October 2023, as an essential component of TTPS commitment to maintaining the highest 
standards of professionalism, ethics, accountability and performance. 
Composition: 
1. Commissioner of Police – Chairman 
2. Deputy Commissioner of Police ‘Administration and Support’ – Deputy Chairman / 
Member 
3. Deputy Commissioner of Police ‘Intelligence and Investigations’ – Deputy Chairman / 
Member 
4. Deputy Commissioner of Police ‘Operations’ – Deputy Chairman / Member 
5. Manager ‘Monitoring, Evaluation and Compliance Unit’ – Member  
6. Assistant Commissioner of Police – The ACP will rotate but he/she must not be the line 
ACP for the officer/s who is/are before the Committee 
Purpose: 
a. Policy Formulation: - Advise on the formulation of policies for the efficient management 
of the TTPS arising from information brought to the Committee’s attention; 
b. Reviewing, Monitoring and Evaluation: - Participate in the review, monitoring and 
evaluation of activities identified in the Strategic and Operational Plans by holding persons 
to account; 
c. Transparency and Accountability - To enforce further transparency, integrity, impartiality, 
fairness and hold officers accountable whilst maintaining strict confidentiality regarding 
sensitive information; and  
d. Assess - Review the strategic direction of the TTPS in achieving its qualitative and 
quantitative results and outcomes. 
Meetings: 
A quorum of three (3) committee members will constitute a sitting, but either the Chairman or 
Deputy Chairman must be present at all times. Meetings will take place at least once per quarter.
