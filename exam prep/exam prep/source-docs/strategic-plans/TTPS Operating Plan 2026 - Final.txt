1.0 
 INTRODUCTION 
 
The Trinidad and Tobago Police Service (TTPS) Operating Plan 2026 expands on the action items outlined in 
the TTPS Strategic Plan  2025-2027 by clearly defining operational targets for a one -year period  and 
identifying those charged with functional responsibility for achieving these targets. Specific performance 
targets have been established for the expected outcomes and enhanced accountability measures have been 
introduced to enable achievement of our stated objectives. 
The plan articulates our roadmap for the second year of the three-year Strategic Planning period. It includes 
four (4) Strategic Priority Areas: 
i. Community Partnership 
ii. Organizational Development 
iii. Operational Excellence 
iv. Public Safety 
These Strategic Priority Areas, as represented in Figure 1 below, provide a comprehensive framework for 
the organization’s delivery of high-quality service to its internal and external customers.  
The Operating Plan 2026 signifies the leadership's confidence in the strategies that will be implemented to 
ensure safety and security in every community, while also demonstrating our determination to overcome 
any challenges encountered.

Financial Position 
According to the World Bank's Global Economic Prospects report published in January 2025, global growth 
was projected to remain steady at 2.7% to 3.0% in 2026, maintaining the pace observed in 2025. The United 
Nations' World Economic Situation and Prospect s (WESP) and UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD) 
reports, released at the end of 2025, forecasts global economic growth to contract moving from 2.8% for 
2025 to 2.6% in 2026. This projected slowdown in growth is expected on account of trade barriers and 
geopolitical uncertainties.  
Based on the International Monetary Fund's (IMF) projections in late 2024, Trinidad and Tobago was 
forecasted to register real Gross Domestic Product (GDP) growth rates of 2.4% in 2025 and 2.2% in 2026. 
However, the updated IMF 2025 October report projected a 1.0% downgrade due to lower than expected 
energy production uncertainties. Notwithstanding, real GDP is expected to grow at a slightly higher rate of 
1.2% to 1.5% in 2026. These projections however hinge on favourable market conditions and a strengthened 
National Security apparatus that stimulates business (local and international) and tourism opportunities 
thereby impacting positively on the development trajectory.  
The Government of the Republic of Trinidad and Tobago (GORTT) recognizes the vital role National Security 
plays in the wider society and economy by ensuring a safe and secure environment. In this regard, it has 
maintained a significant budgetary allocation in which the TTPS has received a slight increase for Fiscal Year 
2026 from $2.6B in Fiscal 2025 to $2.61B as it plays a critical role in law enforcement and the Nation’s 
stability (see Figure 3). 
In this context the TTPS must continue to exercise prudent financial management and effective execution 
of its operations to focus on critical strategic areas that require priority attention.

RESULTS BASED MANAGEMENT AND MONITORING  
The Performance Targets for each strategic priority area below are the results which are expected through 
implementation of the related core strategies and action items.  Performance Targets therefore represent 
outcome level results and are subsequently broken down into action items that create the foundatio n for 
success to be achieved. 
Due to the interconnected nature of the strategies being pursued, Performance Targets are in many cases 
connected to several action items. For tracking and reporting purposes (to avoid duplication) each target 
will be directly pinned to one primary outcome only. 
Priority action items for 20 26 are highlighted in this document, it therefore addresses action items that 
arose in the 2025-2027 Strategic Plan which will be the focus for Year 2 of the Strategic Planning period. It 
should also be noted that several performance targets and action items are heavily contingent on the timely 
release of sufficient budgetary allocations to the TTPS.

 CONCLUSION 
The Trinidad and Tobago Police Service recognizes the challenges posed by increasing demands for policing 
services amidst limited resources. Yet, we remain steadfast in our mission to safeguard all who reside in or 
visit our twin-island republic. Guided by our Operati ng Plan 2026, we will pursue our strategic objectives 
with unwavering passion, professionalism, and enhanced collaboration with our stakeholders. 
The progress achieved in 2025 demonstrates that meaningful change is possible, yet we recognize that 
sustained focus and effort are essential to maintain momentum and drive continuous improvement. With 
confidence in our capacity and determination in our purpose, we reaffirm our enduring pledge to Protect 
and Serve, upholding the highest standards of Professionalism, Respect, Integrity, Dignity, and Excellence.
